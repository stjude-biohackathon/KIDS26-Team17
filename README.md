## Project Profile

- **Project name:** Where Healing Happens: Mapping Mental Health in Childhood Cancer
- **Question, problem, or opportunity:** We are exploring how environmental and socio-economic factors surrounding where pediatric cancer patients live may be associated with their mental health and quality-of-life outcomes. Specifically, we will examine relationships between environmental exposures and neighborhood characteristics—such as air pollution, noise, green space, land use, Child Opportunity Index, and other SES measures—and PROMIS and MSPSS scores. The goal is to determine whether spatial patterns or environmental stressors may help explain differences in psychological well-being and social support among pediatric patients.

## Vision and Mission

- **Vision:**	With this project, we aim to create a proof of concept workflow and interactive dashboards to visualize potential geographic patterns, spatial clustering & hotspot visualizations, correlation analyses between PROMIS / MSPSS scores. 
- **Mission:**	With this project, we hope to explore how socio-economic and area/neighborhood level factors affect mental health & quality of life for children undergoing active cancer treatments.

## About

Clinical care for pediatric cancer patients is centered around biological- and treatment-related factors, which often overlooks the local environmental and socio-economic factors that may help explain mental health and quality of life outcomes. Local conditions that will be included are air quality, noise, access to green space, and broader measures of opportunity like housing, education, and economic stability. These local conditions can meaningfully shape stress, resilience, and recovery that may affect the healing process for children undergoing cancer treatment. By combining deidentified clinical data with publicly available geospatial datasets, this project aims to surface those correlate these environmental and socio-economic factors in this population. Ultimately, the end product of this biohackathon could help clinical teams identify which patients or communities might benefit most from additional psychosocial support to improve equity for mental health care.
